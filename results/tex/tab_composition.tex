Mean Cliff's $\delta$ over the 12 CEC2022 functions with a 95\% percentile bootstrap interval (20{,}000 resamples). $\delta$ is Cliff's delta of arm A against arm B, A being the changed, new or first-named arm; negative values favour A. Bold marks an interval that excludes zero. $^{\S}$: the Student-$t$ or BCa interval includes zero (and so does its adjusted version). $^{\ddagger}$: the Bonferroni-adjusted interval over the four blocks of the row (98.75\%) includes zero. $^{\circ}$: the percentile, BCa or Student-$t$ interval excludes zero with its bound nearest zero within 0.02 of it (a tie). Twelve functions give too little power for Holm-corrected significance to decide anything here, so the decisions read the intervals. They follow the amended rule: a part is dropped when no interval lies above zero and it is either negligible in every block ($|\delta|<0.147$) or has an interval below zero; otherwise it is kept. That rule was adopted after the output of the originally coded rule on these data had been seen. The original rule dropped a part only if it was negligible in every block; it would have kept opposition-based initialisation and the L\'evy perturbation, and agrees with the amended rule on the other four single changes. Under the amended rule the archive and the success-history memory earn their place; opposition-based initialisation, the L\'evy perturbation and the $p$ annealing do not. Opposition-based initialisation never helps, and removing it changes performance by a negligible amount (at $D{=}10$ with 15{,}000 evaluations $-0.066$ $[-0.122,\,-0.012]$, inside the band; the Student-$t$ bound reaches zero, $[-0.131,\,-0.000]$). The $p$ annealing is negligible in every block only at the 0.147 margin. Neither verdict of negligibility survives the Bonferroni-adjusted Student-$t$ interval: removing opposition-based initialisation gives $[-0.154,\,+0.023]$ there, and removing the $p$ annealing $[-0.149,\,+0.096]$ at $D{=}10$ with 15{,}000 evaluations and $[-0.146,\,+0.166]$ at $D{=}20$ with 15{,}000 evaluations. Under those intervals the rule would keep every part by default, so every decision here is directional; the frozen configuration does not change. The last row (stage E11, added after the original rule's output had been seen) removes all three together. The first row re-introduces the gate into the gate-free configuration; its harm at the competition budgets is directionally consistent, within the CEC2017 function family, with the large-budget result on CEC2017, for a greedier gate: here it is scheduled on generations counted from the initial population, so under population reduction it closes after about 40\% of the budget rather than half and opens on about 12--13\% of updates rather than 15.3\%. At 15{,}000 evaluations the two configurations are not separated.
