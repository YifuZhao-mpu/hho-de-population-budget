\begin{flushleft}\footnotesize
CEC2017 at $D=30$, 29 functions, 30 runs per cell; suite, dimension, population size and seed protocol are identical everywhere, so the two budget blocks differ only in budget. $\delta$ is Cliff's delta of arm A against arm B (A the changed, new or first-named arm), computed per function over the 30 runs of each arm and averaged over the functions; negative values favour A. Here A is the configuration with the component removed (for the interactions: the first-named gate effect minus the second). $\bar\delta$ is the mean over the 29 functions; the intervals are a 95\% percentile bootstrap interval over functions (20{,}000 resamples) and a Student-$t$ interval ($t_{28}$). $p_{\text{Holm}}$ on the $\delta$ scale is a two-sided signed-rank test of the 29 per-function deltas, Holm-corrected within each of the three families of four; on mean errors it is the signed-rank test of Table~\ref{S-tab:gatefactorial} on per-function mean errors, Holm-corrected over the four effects of a budget (not defined for the interactions); $^{*}$ marks $p_{\text{Holm}}<0.05$. The two scales disagree on one contrast, the gate with L\'evy off at 300{,}000 evaluations (0.002 against 0.169). Verdicts: negl.\ = the whole interval lies inside $(-m,+m)$; $\neq0$ = the interval excludes zero; incon.\ = it includes zero and reaches outside the band; tie = it excludes zero with a bound within 0.02 of zero. Each verdict holds for the unadjusted percentile, BCa and Student-$t$ intervals alike. The margin 0.147 is the threshold below which \citet{romano2006appropriate} call an effect negligible in survey data; it is a convention, not a property of optimiser error, which is why the verdicts at 0.10 and 0.05 are given beside it. The equivalence margin and the $\delta$-scale analysis were added after the mean-error results existed. A tie is reported for the budget interaction with L\'evy off (percentile $[-0.184,\,-0.007]$, Student-$t$ $[-0.187,\,-0.000]$), whose Bonferroni-adjusted intervals also include zero (Student-$t$ $[-0.216,\,+0.028]$, percentile $[-0.207,\,+0.018]$): the unadjusted intervals exclude zero, but the bound nearest zero lies within 0.02 of it, which Section~\ref{subsec:stats} treats as a tie, so the budget dependence is not established with L\'evy off. $^{\dagger}$: the verdict does not survive the family adjustment; under the Bonferroni-adjusted (98.75\%) Student-$t$ interval of its family of four (Table~\ref{S-tab:supp_gate_intervals}) it is inconclusive. This applies to four verdicts of negligibility, at 0.147 the gate with L\'evy off at 15{,}000 evaluations ($[-0.167,\,+0.069]$), the L\'evy perturbation with the gate off at 15{,}000 evaluations ($[-0.152,\,+0.076]$) and the gate $\times$ L\'evy interaction at 300{,}000 ($[-0.153,\,+0.107]$); at 0.10 the L\'evy perturbation with the gate off at 300{,}000 ($[-0.090,\,+0.111]$). Every other verdict holds under the adjusted intervals as well.
