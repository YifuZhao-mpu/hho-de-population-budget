Intervals are 95\% percentile bootstrap intervals, over functions for the suites and over runs for the design problems. Status: \emph{measured}, CEC2017, the diagnostic suite; \emph{exploratory}, CEC2014 blocks run after the confirmatory analysis; \emph{in-sample}, CEC2022, the suite the rule was selected on (the gate-free engine before the composition step; LPSR-18D against LPSR-6D, sign reversed from the analysis behind Table~\ref{S-tab:poprule}); \emph{registered, exploratory}, the control on the five design problems (Table~\ref{tab:e16}), registered before it ran and exploratory by its registration; \emph{interpolated}, a band between measurements, for which no recommendation is made; \emph{literature}, the initial population rates that \citet{tanabe2015tuning} obtained by tuning restart L-SHADE jointly with its other parameters on CEC2014 F1--F16 at $D=2$, 10 and 20 (their Table~I), not results of this study. The rows come from different problems, dimensions and engines, and $B/D$ is confounded with $D$ and with the problem class; this is not a controlled sweep. From 300 to 1{,}700 evaluations per variable every measured row favours $6D$ ($\delta$ from $-0.97$ to $-0.42$, $D$ from 9 to 50); at $10^4$ per variable L-SHADE's $18D$ is better in 3 of 4 blocks ($\delta$ from $+0.21$ to $+0.52$). Design-problem qualifiers: RC06 compares violations only; on RC14 both arms end about 9\% above the best-known value; on RC01 the feasibility difference is not significant. At 300 per variable even $6D$ may be too large for the repaired configuration: SEHHO-COBL with its fixed $N=30$ beats it at $D=50$ with 15{,}000 evaluations ($+0.50$ on CEC2017, $+0.37$ on CEC2014, SEHHO-COBL-R against SEHHO-COBL; the two differ in five respects). SEHHO-COBL-R fixes $N_{\text{init}}=6D$ at every budget and does not follow this guide.
